COLLEGE RULE BOOK — KNOWLEDGE BASE FOR AI CHATBOT
=============================================================
SOURCE-BASED PROJECT CORPUS
Compiled from the university regulations and 2024 examination-rule material
provided for this project.

IMPORTANT SOURCE NOTE
The supplied project material states that the intended corpus is 6,000+ words,
but the uploaded transcript contains an incomplete/truncated copy of the
supposed full rulebook. This file therefore preserves the available
source-backed rules and adds clearly labelled project test clauses and
explanatory notes. Explanatory notes are not presented as additional official
university regulations.

=============================================================
1. INTRODUCTION AND SCOPE
=============================================================

This Rule Book knowledge base is designed for a question-answering system
over university regulations and examination rules. The system is intended to
answer student questions from the supplied corpus, show the passages used to
form an answer, indicate when the corpus does not contain enough information,
and identify deliberate or source-level contradictions.

The available source material covers admission, attendance, examination,
grading, supplementary semester, CGPA improvement, unfair means, conduct and
discipline, library, hostel, counselling, fees, grievances, training and
placement, branch or programme change, degree eligibility and student
services.

The source material also states that some sections may contain OCR errors,
duplicated text, incomplete sentences or missing attachments. Such material
must not be silently converted into a definite rule. Where a fee amount,
deadline, form, committee procedure or other detail is missing, the chatbot
should say that the available material does not specify it.

For this reason, this corpus distinguishes between a rule stated in the source,
an explanatory note, and a deliberately planted test contradiction.

2. ADMISSION AND ELIGIBILITY
=============================================================

REGULATION 3 — ADMISSION

The Admission Committee is responsible for determining eligibility for
admissions, subject to the applicable university process.

Admissions are made after a due process of advertisement through the
university website and/or other prescribed channels.

First-year admissions are conducted before the beginning of the academic year.
Diploma-qualified candidates may receive lateral entry into the second year of
an applicable programme, subject to the relevant admission provisions.

Admitted students are required to pay prescribed tuition and other applicable
fees and submit required documents. If document verification subsequently
reveals incorrect, false or mismatched information, admission may be affected
according to the applicable rules.

Transfer from another university is governed by the applicable academic
regulations.

REGULATION 21 — ADMISSION PROCEDURE

The university's admission procedure includes principles such as national
integration and equal opportunity. The source also refers to dual-degree,
integrated and interdisciplinary programmes.

A student cannot register for a full-time programme while already registered
in another full-time programme, subject to the applicable rules.

An Admission Assistance Cell is established to assist with admissions.
Applications are scrutinized and merit/eligibility requirements are applied
through the prescribed process.

EXPLANATORY NOTE:
The source does not provide every admission cut-off, every programme-specific
eligibility condition, or every fee amount. The chatbot should not invent
these details.

RULES & REGULATIONS FOR EXAMINATIONS (2024) — ATTENDANCE

Attendance in class is compulsory and is monitored.

The available source material describes 75% attendance as the normal
requirement for examination eligibility. Theory and practical attendance may
be considered separately where the applicable regulation requires it.

For special reasons such as prolonged illness, an attendance deficiency may
be condoned, but only within the limits and approval process specified by the

The source separately states that Pharmacy programmes require 80% attendance

A student with attendance deficiency should contact the department or

EXPLANATORY NOTE:

5. EXAMINATION RULES
=============================================================

RULES & REGULATIONS FOR EXAMINATIONS (2024)

5.1 ADMISSION OF CANDIDATE TO AN EXAMINATION

Only regular candidates satisfying the applicable academic, registration and
attendance requirements are admitted to university examinations.

5.2 ATTENDANCE REQUIREMENT

Attendance is compulsory and monitored. Eligibility is subject to the
attendance requirement applicable to the programme and the relevant
examination regulations.

5.3 COURSE REGISTRATION

No credits are awarded for an unregistered course. A student who fails to
register for a course cannot assume that attendance or assessment in that
course automatically produces academic credit.

5.4 EXAMINATION COMPONENTS

Examination components may include quizzes, class tests, assignments, viva,
mid-semester examination and end-semester examination components according to
the prescribed academic structure.

5.5 MST AND ESE SCHEDULING

MST and ESE dates are scheduled according to the academic calendar. Students
should follow the official academic calendar for the applicable semester.

5.6 EXAMINATION FORM AND FEES

Students must submit the examination form and pay prescribed examination fees
within the stipulated time.

The supplied source does not include a complete table of all examination fees
or every deadline.

5.7 BRANCH UPGRADATION

Branch upgradation after the first year is based on factors including earned
credits, CGPA and admission-related provisions.

5.8 GRADING SCHEMES

Detailed grading schemes are prescribed for different academic areas,
including Engineering, AHSS, Commerce and Management.

5.9 SGPA AND CGPA CALCULATION

SGPA and CGPA are calculated according to the prescribed formula and recorded
to two decimal places.

5.10 MARKS DEFICIENCY CONDONATION

Up to 5 marks of deficiency may be condoned in a maximum of two courses,
subject to the applicable rules.

5.11 CGPA BENEFIT IN BORDERLINE CASES

A 0.01 CGPA benefit may be granted in specified borderline cases, subject to
the applicable university provisions.

5.12 DIVISION REQUIREMENTS FOR B.TECH

CGPA 7.5 or above: First Division with Distinction.
CGPA 6.5 to below 7.5: First Division.
CGPA 5.0 to below 6.5: Second Division.
Minimum CGPA 5.0 is required for degree award.

6. GRADING, SGPA AND CGPA
=============================================================

REGULATION 31 — GRADING SYSTEM

The grading system evaluates student performance using letter grades and
associated grade points.

O = 10
A+ = 9
A = 8
B+ = 7
B = 6
C = 5
D = 4
F = 0 (Fail)

An F grade means failure in a course. Course credits are earned only when the
student obtains the required passing grade according to the applicable
regulations.

SGPA and CGPA are rounded to two decimal places and recorded in that form.

PP and NP may be used as certifications for extra-academic activities.

Students may take additional audit courses from the fifth semester onward,
subject to Faculty Council or other applicable provisions.

REGULATION 28 — SUPPLEMENTARY SEMESTER

The university conducts a Supplementary Semester once a year so that students

8. CGPA IMPROVEMENT
=============================================================

REGULATION 34 — CGPA IMPROVEMENT

Students who have secured a CGPA between 5.0 and 6.5 after completing the
prerequisite credits may be eligible for CGPA improvement under the applicable
regulation.

Eligible students may be permitted to withdraw grades in a maximum of five
subjects of their choice.

Eligibility is limited to within one year of passing the UG/PG examination.

A written application must be submitted to the Chairperson of the Departmental
Council.

The source specifies a ₹100 judicial-stamp-paper affidavit concerning the use
of surrendered marks/grades.

Regular classes are not required for the improvement attempt according to the
available source, but the student must take the prescribed evaluation tests.

The student must pass all selected subjects in the registered improvement
attempt.

If an improvement attempt lowers the overall CGPA, the original result is
retained.

EXPLANATORY NOTE:
The chatbot should distinguish "eligible for improvement" from "guaranteed
CGPA increase". The rule permits an improvement process; it does not guarantee
a particular final CGPA.

9. UNFAIR MEANS AND EXAMINATION MISCONDUCT
=============================================================

REGULATION 56 — UNFAIR MEANS IN THE EXAMINATION

No candidate shall use unfair means or indulge in disorderly conduct in
connection with an examination.

CATEGORY A — RELATIVELY MINOR OFFENCES

Examples include talking, unauthorized seat change, possession of
insignificant material and asking questions inappropriately during an
examination.

CATEGORY B

Examples include revealing identity in an answer book, writing solutions on a
question paper and possession of material covered by the relevant category.

CATEGORY C

Copying, using caught material or passing slips/questions may fall under this
category and can result in cancellation of the examination or other prescribed
penalty.

CATEGORY D

Serious misconduct can include obstructing invigilators, smuggling answer
books and deliberately interfering with examination procedures.

CATEGORY E

Very serious offences can include impersonation, dangerous weapons in
examination premises and similar serious misconduct.

Cases not specifically covered may be decided by the Vice-Chancellor on the
recommendation of the Unfair Means Committee, as applicable.

EXPLANATORY NOTE:
The exact punishment for a particular incident should not be invented from the
category name alone. The applicable regulation and disciplinary decision
control the final consequence.

10. CONDUCT AND DISCIPLINE
=============================================================

REGULATION 5 — CONDUCT AND DISCIPLINE

Students are required to conduct themselves in a manner befitting students of
the university.

Ragging in any form is not permitted within or outside the university where
covered by the applicable anti-ragging provisions.

Examples of gross violations or acts of indiscipline include lack of courtesy
or decorum, possession/consumption/distribution of alcoholic drinks or banned
drugs, mutilation or unauthorized possession of library materials, noisy or
unseemly behaviour disturbing peace, hacking computer systems/hardware/software,
and other acts considered gross indiscipline.

Punishment depends on the gravity of the offence and may include:
- reprimand;
- fine;
- expulsion from hostel;
- debarment from examination;
- temporary rustication;
- outright expulsion.

Reprimanding authorities may include:
- hostel matters: Chief Warden;
- department/classroom matters: Head of the concerned Department;
- other cases: Dean of Students.

Major disciplinary cases involving punishment beyond a simple reprimand are
considered through the prescribed process.

A student punished for an offence has a right to appeal to the Vice-Chancellor
through the applicable procedure.

21. SOURCE-CITATION BEHAVIOUR
=============================================================

Every answered response should provide the section(s) used.

A citation should identify the relevant section and show a short evidence
passage.

The similarity score is a retrieval transparency measure. It should not be
described as proof that an answer is legally or academically correct.

A high similarity score can still be associated with an irrelevant passage.
The final answer should therefore be grounded in the retrieved text.

22. CONFLICT DETECTION
=============================================================

The project requires three deliberate contradictions to test whether the
system can recognize disagreement rather than confidently selecting one rule.

The following clauses are deliberately labelled as TEST CLAUSES. They are
project test data and are NOT presented as official university regulations.

TEST CONTRADICTION A — ATTENDANCE

TEST CLAUSE A1:
"Students shall be eligible for the examination when attendance is at least
75%, subject to the applicable attendance rules."

TEST CLAUSE A2:
"Students shall be eligible for the examination only when attendance is at
least 80%."

These clauses deliberately disagree. A question asking for the attendance
threshold should trigger a conflict response when both clauses are retrieved.

TEST CONTRADICTION B — LIBRARY FINE

TEST CLAUSE B1:
"The overdue charge is ₹1 per book per day."

TEST CLAUSE B2:
"The overdue charge is ₹2 per book per day."

These clauses deliberately disagree. A question asking for the overdue fine
should trigger a conflict response rather than silently choosing one amount.

TEST CONTRADICTION C — BRANCH CHANGE

TEST CLAUSE C1:
"Branch change may be considered at the beginning of the third semester based
on seats and Semester II CGPA."

TEST CLAUSE C2:
"Branch change is not permitted after completion of the first semester."

These clauses deliberately disagree about the permitted timing. The system
should report the contradiction and cite both clauses.

26. SYSTEM DESIGN NOTES
=============================================================

The knowledge base is intended to be consumed by a retrieval-based question
answering service.

The service should:
1. load this text file;
2. split it into meaningful sections;
3. create vector embeddings for retrieval;
4. retrieve the most similar sections for a question;
5. evaluate whether enough evidence exists;
6. identify conflict clauses where applicable;
7. return the answer type;
8. return citations and similarity scores.

The three response types required by the project are:

ANSWERED
The corpus contains sufficient supporting information.

NOT_COVERED
The corpus is silent or the retrieval confidence is below the configured
threshold.

CONFLICT
Two relevant clauses in the corpus disagree. The system should show both
pieces of evidence.

29. RULE-BY-RULE INTERPRETATION GUIDE FOR THE QA SYSTEM
=============================================================

This section is a retrieval-oriented interpretation guide. It does not create
new university regulations. Its purpose is to help the QA system connect
natural student questions with the rulebook sections that contain the relevant
information.

ADMISSION QUESTIONS

A student may ask whether admission is possible, whether lateral entry exists,
whether documents are required, whether transfer is permitted, or whether a
student can simultaneously register in two full-time programmes.

For such questions, the relevant evidence is normally found in the Admission
and Eligibility section and the Admission Procedure subsection. The system
should cite those passages instead of responding from general assumptions
about university admissions.

Questions about exact cut-offs, exact marks, exact dates, or programme-specific
requirements should only be answered when those values are explicitly present
in the corpus. The existence of a general admission regulation does not mean
that every admission detail is contained in the rulebook.

REGISTRATION QUESTIONS

Questions about course registration should be connected to the registration
section and the examination rules. The important source-backed principle is
that course registration is mandatory and that no credits are awarded for an
unregistered course.

A question such as "Will I receive credits if I attended a course but never
registered for it?" should therefore be answered from the stated registration
rule. The system should not invent an exception unless the corpus contains one.

ATTENDANCE QUESTIONS

Attendance questions can have multiple dimensions. A student might ask for
the normal percentage, ask about practical attendance, ask about a medical
reason, ask about examination eligibility, or ask about a particular
programme.

The source states a normal 75% attendance requirement and separately identifies
an 80% requirement for Pharmacy programmes. It also says that deficiency may
be condoned for special reasons such as prolonged illness, within applicable
limits and an approval process.

The system should therefore pay attention to programme names. It should not
answer an 80% Pharmacy requirement as though it automatically applied to every
student.

Similarly, it should not turn the existence of medical condonation into a
guarantee that every illness automatically permits examination attendance.
The source describes condonation as subject to limits and approval.

EXAMINATION QUESTIONS

Examination questions may concern eligibility, forms, fees, components,
scheduling, marks deficiency, grading, branch upgradation or division.

When the student asks for an exact examination date, the system should look
for an explicit date. If the corpus only says that dates are scheduled
according to the academic calendar, it should not invent a calendar date.

When the student asks about an examination fee, the system should distinguish
between the rule that fees must be paid and an actual fee amount. A requirement
to pay a prescribed fee does not provide the amount.

GRADING QUESTIONS

Grade questions should use the grade-point table. The source maps O to 10,
A+ to 9, A to 8, B+ to 7, B to 6, C to 5, D to 4 and F to 0.

A question asking what F means should be answered directly: F means fail.
A question asking for a student's calculated CGPA, however, requires the
student's grades and credits, which are not contained in this corpus unless
provided separately by the user.

The system should also distinguish SGPA from CGPA. The source says both are
calculated according to the prescribed formula and recorded to two decimal
places, but the complete formula is not reproduced in the available corpus.
Therefore an exact formula should not be invented if the question requires
details not present here.

SUPPLEMENTARY SEMESTER QUESTIONS

The supplementary semester is intended to provide a route for eligible
students to clear failed courses. The source states that a maximum of 18
credits may be registered and that students register only for failed courses,
subject to the applicable rules.

A student asking "Can I register for 20 credits?" should not receive a yes
based on a generic assumption. The source specifically states a maximum of
18 credits.

Questions about the exact supplementary fee, registration deadline or course
availability require information that may not be in this corpus. In those
cases, the system should return NOT COVERED.

CGPA IMPROVEMENT QUESTIONS

CGPA improvement has explicit eligibility conditions in the supplied source.
The stated CGPA range is 5.0 to 6.5. The student may select up to five subjects,
and eligibility is limited to within one year of passing the UG/PG examination.

The source also mentions a written application, a Departmental Council
Chairperson, a ₹100 judicial-stamp-paper affidavit, prescribed evaluation
tests, and retention of the original result if the improvement attempt lowers
the overall CGPA.

The system should not claim that every student can improve a CGPA. The stated
conditions must be considered together.

UNFAIR-MEANS QUESTIONS

Unfair-means questions require careful language. The source divides offences
into Categories A through E and gives examples of conduct associated with
different levels of seriousness.

The existence of categories does not mean that the system should assign a
specific punishment to every individual case unless the source says so.
Punishment can depend on the applicable category, facts and disciplinary
decision.

For questions about a phone, paper, copying, impersonation or obstruction of
an invigilator, the system should retrieve the UFM section and cite the
relevant category or categories.

DISCIPLINE QUESTIONS

The discipline section describes prohibited conduct and possible punishments.
Possible consequences include reprimand, fine, hostel expulsion, examination
debarment, temporary rustication and outright expulsion.

A response should not say that a particular student "will definitely be
expelled" unless the rule explicitly mandates that result. The supplied
material says punishment depends on the gravity of the offence.

LIBRARY QUESTIONS

Library questions often contain exact factual values and are therefore useful
retrieval tests.

The source states that students may borrow five books, with a normal loan
period of 14 days and an overdue charge of ₹1 per book per day. Faculty,
Ph.D. scholars and non-teaching staff have different borrowing limits.

The system should not confuse a student borrowing limit with the Ph.D.
scholar limit. It should also distinguish borrowing from reference-library
use.

HOSTEL QUESTIONS

Hostel questions can concern allotment, rooms, movement, out-passes, fees,
electrical appliances, late coming or penalties.

The supplied source contains several behavioural requirements but does not
contain a complete numerical penalty table. Therefore, if a student asks for
the exact fine for a particular hostel violation and that value is absent, the
correct answer is NOT COVERED.

COUNSELLING QUESTIONS

The counselling section says that counselling is considered integral to
education and describes Faculty Counselors for groups of approximately
20–25 students. It also mentions guidance, psychometric counselling and
development sessions.

The system should not infer a counsellor's personal contact details, office
hours or appointment schedule unless those details are added to the corpus.

FEES QUESTIONS

Fee questions are an important example of why retrieval alone is not enough.
The source says that fees and payment schedules are prescribed and identifies
a committee structure. It also refers to a Table.xls containing tuition fees
and other charges.

Because that table is not included in the available corpus, the system must
not fabricate an exact tuition amount.

A question such as "Does the university have prescribed tuition fees?" can be
answered from the source. A question such as "What exactly is the B.Tech
tuition fee this year?" cannot be answered from the available material.

GRIEVANCE QUESTIONS

The grievance material addresses women's grievances, sexual-harassment
prevention, equal opportunity and gender sensitization.

The corpus supports the existence of grievance mechanisms but does not give
every current contact detail or every procedural form. Such missing
information must be treated as not covered.

PLACEMENT QUESTIONS

The Training and Placement Cell provides placement support, internship
support, career guidance and training and coordinates with industries.

This does not establish a guaranteed placement or a guaranteed salary.
Company-specific eligibility and package information should only be answered
if explicitly included in the corpus.

BRANCH-CHANGE QUESTIONS

Branch change involves availability and merit. The source identifies
Semester II CGPA as a ranking factor for the third-semester process and also
mentions sanctioned seats, admissions and vacancies.

A question asking whether a branch change is guaranteed should therefore be
answered cautiously. The existence of a process is not a guarantee that the
student will receive the requested branch.

The source also states that there is no provision for changing between
different programmes such as B.Tech and BCA/BBA under the described
regulation.

DEGREE-ELIGIBILITY QUESTIONS

Degree award requires completion of required credits, required passing
grades, non-credit/non-gradial requirements, basket requirements, minimum
CGPA, dues clearance and the required disciplinary status.

The minimum CGPA stated by the source is 5.0.

The system should treat these as cumulative requirements. Meeting one
condition does not automatically establish degree eligibility if another
condition remains incomplete.

30. NATURAL-LANGUAGE QUESTION PATTERNS
=============================================================

The QA system should understand common ways students may phrase the same
question.

"How many books may I issue?" is a library borrowing question.
"How many books can students take?" is also a library borrowing question.
"What's the student library limit?" is another formulation of the same
concept.

"Can I sit for the exam with 70% attendance?" is an attendance eligibility
question.
"Is 75 percent compulsory?" is an attendance threshold question.
"Does medical leave help with attendance?" is an attendance-condonation
question.

"What does F mean?" is a grading question.
"How many points is O?" is a grade-point question.
"What CGPA is needed for the degree?" is a degree-eligibility question.

"Can I improve my CGPA?" is a CGPA-improvement question.
"How many subjects can I improve?" asks about the maximum number of subjects.
"How long after passing can I apply?" asks about the stated one-year limit.

"Can I clear my backlogs in supplementary semester?" is a supplementary
semester question.
"What is the maximum supplementary credit load?" asks about the 18-credit
limit.

"Can I change my branch after first year?" is a branch-change question.
"Does second-semester CGPA matter?" is also a branch-change question.

"What happens if I copy?" is an unfair-means question.
"What if I obstruct the invigilator?" concerns serious UFM misconduct.
"Is impersonation allowed?" concerns Category E-type misconduct.

"What is the exact fee?" should trigger a check for whether the corpus
contains an actual amount. The presence of the word "fee" alone is not enough
to decide that a fee amount exists.

=============================================================
31. EVIDENCE-FIRST ANSWERING POLICY
=============================================================

The project is intentionally designed around evidence-first answers.

The system should prefer a short answer grounded in retrieved evidence over a
long answer based on general university knowledge.

If the relevant passage says "normally", the answer should preserve that
qualification.

If the relevant passage says "may", the answer should not change it to
"will".

If the relevant passage says "subject to applicable rules", that condition
should remain visible in the response.

If the source mentions a missing attachment, the system should say that the
attachment is missing rather than pretending it has read the attachment.

If two retrieved clauses conflict, the system should expose both instead of
selecting one silently.

If no relevant evidence is found, the system should say NOT COVERED.

=============================================================
32. QUALITY-CONTROL CHECKLIST
=============================================================

Before considering a response correct, check:

1. Did the answer come from the rulebook corpus?
2. Is the relevant section cited?
3. Is the wording consistent with the source?
4. Did the answer preserve important conditions such as "subject to"
   applicable rules?
5. Did the system avoid inventing a fee, date, phone number or procedure?
6. If contradictory clauses were relevant, did the system flag the conflict?
7. If the corpus was silent, did the system return NOT COVERED?
8. Is the similarity score shown as retrieval evidence rather than absolute
   proof?
9. Does the answer avoid claiming that a project test clause is an official
   university rule?
10. Can a reviewer inspect the exact passage used?

=============================================================
34. FINAL PROJECT PRINCIPLE
=============================================================

The central design principle of this Rulebook QA System is:

DO NOT GUESS.

A useful rulebook assistant is not simply a system that produces fluent
answers. It must distinguish between evidence, missing information and
conflicting information.

When the corpus supports the answer, cite it.

When the corpus is silent, say so.

When two rules disagree, show both.

When the source is incomplete, preserve that limitation.

This behaviour is the core feature being demonstrated by the project.
